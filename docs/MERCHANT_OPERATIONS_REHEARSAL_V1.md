# Merchant Operations Rehearsal V1

## Product decision

**User problem:** completing a readiness assessment does not prove that a merchant can make a calm, safe first response when fulfilment changes, an order is cancelled, or a suspicious request arrives.

**User job:** rehearse one bounded operating incident and leave with an ordered first-response sequence before handling a real customer situation.

**Interaction:** the existing Merchant Readiness Desk assessment produces its plan, then the merchant chooses one of three fixed incident scenarios: delivery delay, cancellation, or suspicious request.

**Output:** a scenario-specific, three-step response sequence and a safe handoff to the existing Safety Center where appropriate.

**Repeat-use loop:** return when an operational process changes, before a new staff member handles orders, or to rehearse a different incident. The tool retains no prior result.

**Differentiation:** this deepens the Desk into a practical operating rehearsal. It is neither an additional static merchant checklist nor payment activation, customer-support automation, or legal/tax advice.

**Whitepaper contribution:** moves PioneerHub from readiness awareness to a small, repeatable operational-safety loop for merchants, while retaining the product's privacy and no-payment boundary.

**Maintenance burden:** three fixed local scenarios plus links to existing safety guidance. No feed, account, customer record, API, external submission, or merchant directory is introduced.

**Future economic path:** demonstrated use could justify owner-approved merchant operations education or a verification service later. V1 makes no offer, pricing, payment, or commercial commitment.

## Limitations and success definition

The rehearsal does not contact a customer, validate a merchant or transaction, determine legal/tax obligations, recommend accepting Pi, integrate a wallet, or provide financial advice. It cannot resolve an actual dispute.

The useful action is a completed incident-response rehearsal: the merchant chooses a bounded scenario and sees its ordered first-response sequence. It is a semantic product outcome only while MUA aggregation remains parked.
